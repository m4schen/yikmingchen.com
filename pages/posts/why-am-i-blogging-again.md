I haven't posted anything on my blog for over two years, so why am I back?

The thought first came to me when I realized that I couldn't finish an email on my own, no matter which language I used.

At first, I just struggled to come up with proper words without relying on polished AI-generated text. But eventually, I found that sometimes I couldn't even organize a complete sentence. It made me reflect on how deeply I'd come to rely on AI for writing, to the point where I struggled to express myself without it.

"Is that really a problem? Sounds like you didn't rely on Grammarly or translation before."

I did, but I see those things differently. With those tools, I was still writing my own sentences. But hey, look at how I was using AI: I often asked it to rewrite the entire paragraphs, whether to make them more professional, soften the tone, or meet certain requirements. Sometimes I simply didn't have the time or patience to rewrite them myself; sometimes I couldn't make my words look better.

The generated text was often good, many even better than what I could have written. But something that felt like mine was slowly being ground away. I was losing not just my own words, but also the practice of finding them.

AI makes writing more important, yet it also makes it easier for me to stop writing.

There's an irony here. As a software engineer, I use LLMs almost every day. Though we don't need to put as much effort into prompting the model as we did two years ago, the ability to clearly articulate what we want still matters. In software development, clearly defining a feature, its boundaries, and its requirements can save us a lot of time revising an agent's output. That's also why I find plugins like "grill-me" interesting: they push us to clarify what we actually want before jumping into implementation.

Writing is also a way of thinking. Sometimes an idea seems clear in my head, but when I try to put it into words, I realize that I don't fully understand it. Writing forces me to identify the gaps in my thinking and find the right words to express my ideas. Writing things out also helps me learn. I take in a lot of ideas through reading, watching videos, and other sources, but they often feel like scattered dots on a piece of paper. Writing is how I connect those dots and make sense of them.

So here I am, blogging again. I don't expect every post to be perfect, but I want to regain my ability to write on my own. I hope something I write will be useful to someone, someday.

And I want the words to be mine.
